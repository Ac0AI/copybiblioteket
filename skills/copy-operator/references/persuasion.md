# Persuasion and copywriting: usable principles

This is an original practical synthesis from the sources below, not a reproduction of books or a claim that any author endorses this skill. Treat frameworks as hypotheses and prompts for judgment, not proven conversion lifts. Match them to the specific audience, offer, evidence and medium. Don't force every technique into a single asset.

## Robert Cialdini: seven principles

Source: [Influence at Work, Seven Principles of Persuasion](https://www.influenceatwork.com/7-principles-of-persuasion/). His framework describes tendencies in particular contexts, not guarantees for an individual audience or campaign.

1. **Reciprocity:** Give the reader something genuinely useful, such as a practical guide or relevant answer. It must stand on its own; a free resource does not obligate a purchase. Do not call something free if hidden conditions apply.
2. **Scarcity:** Explain a real limit when it matters to the decision, such as capacity or a verifiable deadline. Verify the quantity, duration and conditions; omit the tactic if no real limit exists.
3. **Authority:** Make relevant expertise legible with actual qualifications, experience, methodology or independent evidence. A founder's experience is not third-party validation; a logo, certification or expert endorsement needs permission and accurate context.
4. **Commitment and consistency:** Help the reader take a voluntary next step that aligns with their stated goal, for example compare options before booking a call. Don't invent previous commitments or use a small action as a trap for an unrelated obligation.
5. **Liking:** Write in a credible, approachable voice and address the reader's genuine context. Do not pretend to know, agree with or have a relationship with them.
6. **Social proof / consensus:** Show attributable and relevant customer experiences or observed behavior when available, including scope and permission. An isolated testimonial is an individual's experience, not evidence of typical outcomes. Do not imply an endorsement or majority without evidence.
7. **Unity:** Refer to a real shared identity, community or experience only when it exists and matters. Do not manufacture belonging, exploit exclusion or imply the audience has a shared attribute without support.

When a request is for Cialdini-style copy, identify the reader's decision friction and pick one or two principles that can honestly help. For each, identify the evidence needed and produce a usable line; mark a missing example as a placeholder, not as proof. If the user requests it, explain the choice briefly.

## Eugene Schwartz: audience awareness and market context

Sources: [publisher on awareness and sophistication](https://breakthroughadvertisingbook.com/lp4-breakthrough-advertising-system/) and [publisher overview of awareness](https://breakthroughadvertisingbook.com/the-five-states-of-awareness-the-key-to-marketing-that-actually-works/).

Before choosing an opening, estimate what the reader knows: unaware of the problem, problem aware, solution aware, product aware, or already familiar and ready to consider an offer. These are rough planning categories, not diagnostic facts or a mandatory journey. If the evidence is weak, state the assumption and consider contrasting openings for different stages.

- Problem-aware reader: name a recognizable situation, then explain the path to a solution.
- Solution-aware reader: explain how this option works and how it differs where evidence supports it.
- Product-aware reader: address relevant doubts, proof, fit, terms and next action.
- Familiar reader: make the actual offer and conditions easy to assess.

Inspect competing promises and audience skepticism before choosing an angle. Differentiate with a real mechanism, evidence or relevant constraint, not a more extreme promise. An audience and its awareness can vary within the same channel.

## Claude Hopkins: specificity and testing

Source: [Scientific Advertising, 1923, Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/item/23009362/). Its historical examples are context, not current platform rules or modern experimental statistics.

Treat copy as a sales argument: tell the reader what is offered, why it matters and how to act. Use specific facts where they are verified and informative; avoid replacing generalities with invented numbers. For a test, record baseline, changed idea, audience, outcome metric, time period and decision condition. When sample size, attribution or tracking cannot support a conclusion, report the result as inconclusive. A hypothesis is not a finding.

## David Ogilvy: research, headline and brand

Source: [Ogilvy's collection of David Ogilvy quotations](https://www.ogilvy.com/sites/g/files/dhpsjz106/files/pdfdocuments/O23_OGILVY75_Quotations%20of%20David%20Ogilvy_WEB.pdf), particularly the sections on research and headlines. The operational interpretation below is ours.

Learn the product and audience before drafting. Let the headline carry a meaningful, supportable idea and make sure body copy delivers what it promises. Consider how the message contributes to the brand's recognizable voice over time, not merely to a single response metric. Test rather than assume that any particular headline rule works universally.

## Joanna Wiebe: customer language and validation

Sources: [Copyhackers, Joanna Wiebe on copy validation](https://copyhackers.com/how-to-validate-your-copy/) and [conversion copywriting process](https://copyhackers.com/3-part-conversion-copywriting-process-for-newbies/).

Use relevant interviews, reviews, support conversations and other permitted customer material to identify actual problems, objections and phrases. Record source and context; distinguish a small sample from representative research and buyer words from the brand's established voice. Use the research to form message hypotheses and, where practical, validate comprehension with appropriate readers or run a properly measured test. Do not invent a quote or claim a customer validation happened when it did not.

## Alex Hormozi: offer value before wording

Sources: [Acquisition.com, value checklist](https://www.acquisition.com/hubfs/Offer%20Checklists%20-%20PDF%20Downloads/Pricing-Value-Checklist.pdf?hsLang=en) and [official Offers course](https://www.acquisition.com/training/offers). This is a short original application, not the book or the course.

Use the value equation as a qualitative diagnosis: desired outcome and confidence in reaching it increase perceived value; waiting and required effort reduce it. Do not turn it into a numerical score, a pricing formula, or a prediction of sales.

- **Outcome:** Establish the result this buyer actually wants and the part the offer can deliver. A dream outcome is not automatically a supportable promise.
- **Confidence:** Explain relevant proof, delivery method and fit. Identify which doubt remains; use genuine evidence, not added certainty in the wording.
- **Delay:** Separate time to start, time to first useful milestone and time to the eventual result. Only state timeframes the operation supports.
- **Effort and sacrifice:** Make the customer's work and prerequisites explicit. Highlight work the service actually handles rather than implying an effortless result.

His Offers course also covers packaging, bonuses, guarantees, urgency and naming. Apply these only where they improve the actual offer. A bonus should solve a buying or delivery obstacle; a guarantee needs real terms and an owner who can honor it. Do not invent reference prices, discounts or deadlines.

For an offer-led writing task, return the strongest supportable promise, the principal objection, the proof available and the next step. If a better offer requires changing delivery, price or terms, separate that proposal from copy describing the current product. Drafting does not authorize those business changes.

## Practical selection

1. Identify the reader's stage, the job of the asset and the main decision friction; label uncertain assumptions.
2. Choose a clear argument grounded in the offer. Select only the applicable persuasion principle and evidence.
3. Write the piece in the brand's voice. For each material promise, trace the evidence or explicitly flag the gap.
4. Review the actual wording, offer terms and next action; if asked, return a brief note naming the approach, evidence and test hypothesis. Never represent a predicted result as an observed result.

### Worked micro-example, fictional

Input: A service provides a 20-minute introductory call. There is no evidence about results, scarcity or customer testimonials. Reader knows they want help but is comparing options.

- Suitable approach: give a clear voluntary next step and explain what the call covers if known. Do not imply results or social proof.
- Draft: “Boka ett introduktionssamtal på 20 minuter.” Add a factual description of what happens only when verified.
- Gap: whether the call is free or has any other conditions. Verify before adding those details.
