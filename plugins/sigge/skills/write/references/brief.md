# Brand and evidence brief

Create a brief only when it will help this or future work. Reuse verified context and ask only for consequential gaps. A one-off headline rarely needs a full brief. Keep different brands separate; date material and distinguish current facts from old ones.

- **Brand and scope:** name, market, language, links, owner or source.
- **Offer and buyer:** audience and buying context, what is included, price if relevant, action, exclusions, alternatives.
- **Buyer evidence:** actual pains, desired outcomes, objections and decision triggers. Record short permitted quotes with source, date, and context; label inference and hypothesis separately. Do not present a handful of comments as representative research.
- **Positioning:** strongest defensible reason to choose the offer; mechanism, differentiators and constraints. Record what is still unknown.
- **Voice:** genuine writing samples and observations about vocabulary, rhythm, formality and personality; do not turn adjectives into invented claims about an established voice.
- **Proof ledger:** claim, source or evidence, date, scope, status (supported / needs verification / rejected), and permitted wording. A quote needs attribution and permission appropriate to its use. Do not promote an anecdote into a general outcome.
- **Learning log:** date, asset, channel, audience, metric, comparison and outcome. Label sample size and limitations. Record inconclusive tests as inconclusive.

Update the brief only from verified additions or explicit user input. Keep any existing human-written context intact; never carry a client’s private information into another brand's work.
